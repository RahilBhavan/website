Loop 02 / Chapter 10

Redemption, liquidity and settlement

After this chapter: Distinguish asset value, cash availability, secondary sale and redemption.

Watch for this mistake: A 24/7 transfer system guarantees an immediate exit at NAV.

Primary issuance creates an instrument against contributed value. Redemption cancels or returns it according to the product's rules in exchange for cash or assets. Secondary trading transfers an existing instrument between holders. A liquid secondary market and reliable primary redemption can support one another, but neither is guaranteed by the other.

Read who may redeem, with whom, at what valuation, on which days, in what currency, at what minimum size, with which fees and under which suspension rights. An issuer may permit transfers at all hours while banks and asset markets operate limited hours. A token transfer's technical finality does not prove final settlement of the associated cash leg.

Worked stress: a vehicle has $1 million cash and $9 million loans due in six months. Holders request $3 million today. Even if loans are worth their stated value, immediate cash is short $2 million. A forced sale at a discount can turn liquidity pressure into losses. A queue, borrowing facility or redemption gate changes who bears that pressure; each has costs and legal constraints.

Settlement is completion of the obligations. Delivery-versus-payment (DvP) conditions delivery of securities on payment. A truly linked mechanism can reduce principal risk, but it may need prefunding and cannot automatically make an external bank payment atomic. See BIS [8] for the design argument; benefits depend on implementation.

Fresh check R4: cash $800,000, illiquid assets $4.2 million, immediate redemption requests $1.5 million. Find the cash shortfall. Distinguish it from proof of insolvency. What happens if the required asset sale realizes only 70% of book value?

Close the chapter. Try a new case.

A fund has $1.2M cash and receives $2M in immediately payable redemption requests. What is the cash gap? What else is needed to conclude insolvency?

Reveal this case’s solution

The immediate gap is $0.8M. Solvency requires asset values, liabilities and their legal treatment; a timing shortfall alone does not establish that liabilities exceed assets.

Check the core idea

Who supplies value on a secondary sale, and who supplies it on redemption?

A secondary buyer supplies the purchase consideration. The issuer or designated redemption counterparty settles a redemption under its terms.

Loop 02 / Chapter 11

Losses, waterfalls and enforceability

After this chapter: Allocate recovery according to the actual priority of claims.

Watch for this mistake: Senior status or asset backing eliminates losses.

A waterfall specifies who gets paid, in what order, and under what triggers. Senior and junior claims redistribute losses; they do not eliminate them. Fees, secured debt, taxes and enforcement costs can change actual recovery. Model the legal priority rather than assuming every token ranks equally.

Worked simplified waterfall: a vehicle holds $10 million assets financed by $7 million senior claims and $3 million junior equity. Assets recover $8 million, with no costs. Senior receives $7 million and junior $1 million: junior loses 66.67%. If recovery is $6 million, junior gets zero and senior loses $1 million. A small change in gross assets can produce a large change for the residual class.

An on-chain liquidation cannot by itself repossess a building or compel a borrower to pay. Servicers, courts, insolvency administrators and custodians remain important. Ask what happens when the borrower defaults, the custodian fails, a court orders a freeze, a key is lost, or the blockchain and official register disagree.

Compliance is contextual. Identify security or other product classification, offering and distribution route, investor eligibility, transfer rules, custody, AML and sanctions obligations, tax and privacy considerations. Different jurisdictions can treat the same structure differently. This book is not a current jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction legal survey; verify current authoritative rules before applying a design.

What determines who absorbs the first loss?

The enforceable waterfall, claim amounts, asset recovery and any costs or higher-priority obligations. A simplified senior/junior model is only one possible structure.
