## Chapter 19 — Merger Mechanics and Accretion

### Learning objectives

- Calculate shares issued and ownership.
- Build synergy cases.
- Interpret operating-proxy EPS.

### Why it matters

Transaction mechanics translate legal terms into shareholder economics.

### Explanation

\[
Issued=61.033485\text{mm}\times0.3575
=21.819471\text{mm}
\]

\[
PF\ shares=30.733897+21.819471
=52.553368\text{mm}
\]

Basic ownership is approximately 58.48%/41.52%.

Synergy cases:

- 0% of management’s annual run-rate guide.
- 50% illustrative realization.
- 100% of management’s annual run-rate guide.

The guide targets delivery within 24 months, with the majority in the first 12 months; tax status is not established by the registered source.

### Project connection

FY2026E base EPS is $2.1304 and base accretion is +6.52%. The no-synergy case is dilutive.

### Worked example — Illustration

If ACLS standalone NI is $60 million, VECO NI is $10 million, after-tax synergy is $26.25 million, and PF shares are 52.55 million:

\[
PF\ EPS=(60+10+26.25)/52.55
\approx\$1.83
\]

This is an illustration and is not the frozen project output.

### Common mistakes

- Using fully diluted claims as basic ownership.
- Forgetting tax on synergy in an illustrative calculation.
- Ignoring PPA and financing effects.
- Calling scenarios predictions.
- Presenting the sample EPS as a workbook result.

### Checklist

- [ ] Ratio fixed.
- [ ] Share issuance linked.
- [ ] Ownership sums to 100%.
- [ ] Cases labeled.
- [ ] Limitations visible.
- [ ] Illustrations separated from outputs.

Accretion is conditional on assumptions and definitions.
