## Chapter 22 — Claim Ledger and Evidence Chain

### Learning objectives

- Use the claim ledger.
- Reconcile workbook and deliverables.
- Record negative claims.

### Why it matters

Unsupported claims often enter through résumés and pitches rather than models.

### Explanation

The ledger records:

- claim;
- classification;
- source;
- workbook cell;
- locked value;
- as-of date;
- permitted wording;
- prohibited wording.

Examples:

| Claim | Reference |
|---|---|
| Exchange ratio | `Merger!B3` |
| Equity value | `Merger!B12` |
| Ownership | `Merger!B8:B9` |
| Synergy guide | `Merger!B14`, annual run-rate guide; timing and tax status qualified |
| Base FY2026E EPS | `Merger!D83` |
| Base accretion | `Merger!F83` |
| Breakeven synergy | `Merger!B88` |
| Spread | `Spread!F218`, +10.72% as of 2026-08-05, calculated as `(VECO − implied consideration) / implied consideration` |

### Project connection

The ledger prohibits claims of SAMR approval odds, bottoms-up synergies, formal regression, authentic audio mastery, and fresh data beyond the applicable freezes.

### Worked example

Sentence: “The deal offers 10.72% upside.”  
Correct revision:

> “The workbook records a +10.72% spread as of 2026-08-05 under its stated convention, calculated as `(VECO − implied consideration) / implied consideration`; this is not a prediction or recommendation.”

### Common mistakes

- Removing dates for readability.
- Converting calculations into facts.
- Omitting limitations from compressed résumé language.
- Calling a dated spread a current market view.

### Checklist

- [ ] Classify every claim.
- [ ] Add source and cell.
- [ ] Record date.
- [ ] Review prohibited claims.
- [ ] Include the spread convention.

### Takeaways

The claim ledger is a defense against number drift and overclaiming.
