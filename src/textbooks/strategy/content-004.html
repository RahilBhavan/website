Check yourself

Why does STRD pay more than STRK?

It's more junior and its dividends can be skipped forever. STRK is senior to it, its unpaid dividends are owed, and it converts into MSTR, which is worth something by itself.

Which preferred would Strategy most like to keep paying in a crisis, and why?

STRF (and STRE): skipping them triggers compounding at a rate that steps up to 18%. STRD is the one it could skip at no legal cost.

Chapter 10

Every term, and why it's there

A preferred stock is a list of promises. Each promise makes the security more attractive to some buyer, and each one costs Strategy something. Reading the terms this way explains why the five preferreds look so different.

The question to ask of every term is: who does this help, and what does it cost the other side?

Selling below $100 at launch

Every one of them was first sold below its stated amount: STRK at $80, STRF and STRD at $85, STRC at $90 and STRE at €80. That raises the buyer's starting yield above the headline rate (€10 on €80 is 12.5%) without printing a higher rate on the security. It also means Strategy took in less cash than the claim it created. For STRE, €620M raised for a €775M claim. That gap is a real cost, paid up front.

Why a ladder instead of one big preferred

One security would have to be priced for the average buyer. A ladder lets Strategy charge each buyer only what their slice is worth. Cautious money buys STRF at the top and accepts less. Yield-hungry money buys STRD at the bottom and gets more. Strategy collects the difference. The cost is complexity, and a fixed order that can't easily be changed later.

Check yourself

Why would a company ever choose to make its own preferred non-cumulative?

To keep a dividend it can stop without owing it later. Buyers know this and demand a higher rate, so Strategy pays for that flexibility every year.

What does the STRF step-up protect against, and who pays for it?

It protects STRF holders against skipped dividends by making skipping very costly. If Strategy ever did skip, common holders would bear the penalty.

Chapter 11

STRC up close: a dividend that steers a price

STRC is the most important product to understand, for two reasons. It's the largest preferred, about two-thirds of Strategy's annual cash charges. Management can adjust its dividend rate monthly to encourage trading near $100. It remains perpetual preferred equity with market and payment risk. Rate changes require a board decision.

Three 2026 changes around STRC

Buybacks≈ 17m shares ($1.65B) retired from July to Oct 11, 2026, paid for by selling MSTR. Adds net bitcoin per share while STRC trades below $100 × net mNAV ($119 on Oct 11).
Twice-monthly, then dailyMoved from monthly to twice a month in June. If shareholders approve on Oct 28, STRC pays every calendar day from Nov 1 ($1.00 a share for November, $0.03–$0.04 a day).
Rate on hold at 12%Management says it won't recommend changes until STRC shows "sustained, healthy trading near $100."
The risk to understandA variable rate can increase funding costs during stress. The board retains discretion; the price bands do not mechanically reset the coupon. Bitcoin weakness, preferred prices and rate decisions can interact without moving in lockstep. My coverage model (Chapter 27) lets you push STRC to 15% and see what happens to the Reserve.

Check yourself

Why would daily dividends help STRC's price?

More frequent record and payment dates reduce the amount accruing between payments. Strategy argues that this may reduce price swings around dividend dates. It does not eliminate reasons to sell or guarantee price stability.

STRC is at $99 and MSTR's net mNAV is 1.2x. Does buying back STRC add bitcoin per share?

Yes. The rotation adds while m > q: 1.2 > 0.99. It would stop adding only if STRC traded above $120.
