A lot's life

Two traders fixing at once cannot over-price a period. The fix takes a row lock, re-reads the priced quantity, checks that q_priced + q_lot ≤ q_max, and inserts the lot, all in one transaction. With 5,000 MT planned at ±5%, q_max is 5,250 MT. A 300 MT lot on top of 5,000 MT is refused (GT-06).

Append-only

Chapter 5

Mark to market

After this chapter you can
  • Compute MTM and its futures and basis parts.
  • Use the sign convention: purchase +1, sale −1.
  • Explain when MTM is stale or stops.

Mark to market (MTM) compares the price you locked with today's market price. A mark is today's value for futures or basis. The mark must be defined on the same terms as the contract: same futures month, same origin and delivery point. Otherwise MTM measures a difference in definitions, not a change in the market.

MTM = s × Qpriced × (Pmkt − Pavg)   s = +1 purchase, −1 sale
MTMfutures = s × Q × (Fmkt − Favg) × 22.0462
MTMbasis = s × Q × (Bmkt − Bavg) × 22.0462

Worked example: GT-01 (market up)

Marks today: futures 69.00, basis 2.50. Q = 5,000 MT bought at F avg 67.50, basis 2.00.

  • Futures part: 5,000 x (69.00 − 67.50) x 22.0462 = +165,346.50
  • Basis part: 5,000 x (2.50 − 2.00) x 22.0462 = +55,115.50
  • Total = +220,462.00 USD

The split matters. A trader can fix futures well and still lose on basis. The two need different actions.

Unpriced tonnes, over-pricing, and when MTM stops

  • Unpriced quantity = reference quantity − priced. It has no flat-price MTM yet, but it is open risk: sensitivity = unpriced x 22.0462 USD per 1 USc/lb.
  • Reference quantity is planned before loading, then the B/L quantity. If B/L is 4,900 on 5,000 priced, 100 MT is over-priced: 2,204.62 USD per USc/lb with no oil behind it (GT-07).
  • A sale flips the sign: the same lots as a sale give −220,462.00 (GT-08).
  • Fixed-price contracts have no futures/basis split. Record-only products (rice) are excluded from MTM.
  • MTM stops when the oil is physically sold on or transferred to a refinery.
