Glossary

After this chapter you can
  • Look up any term used in the CTRM.

Copied from docs/user-guides/glossary.md at build time, so it matches the repository.

Acceptance
The bank accepts the documents presented under an LC, and the payment clock starts. On "LCs and payments" you record it with "Accept", the "Accepted amount" and "Accepted on".
Average price
The volume-weighted average of a period's confirmed lots. Example: lots of 1,000 MT at 66.00, 2,000 MT at 67.50 and 2,000 MT at 68.25 USc/lb average 67.50 USc/lb. With basis +2.00 the flat price is (67.50 + 2.00) × 22.0462 = 1,532.2109 USD/MT.
B/L
The bill of lading: the carrier's receipt for the cargo. Its date and quantity come in with "Mark loaded" and often start the LC payment clock.
Basis
The premium or discount the seller adds to the CBOT futures price; positive is a premium. It is quoted in USc/lb or USD/MT, and both give the same result.
Basis MTM
The part of MTM that comes from basis moving. Example: 5,000 MT bought at basis 2.00, market basis now 2.50: 5,000 × 0.50 × 22.0462 = +55,115.50 USD.
Basis quote
A broker's bid or offer for basis, logged in the "Basis quote log" whether you took it or not. A quote cannot be edited later.
Cents per pound (USc/lb)
The unit CBOT uses for ZL futures prices. See the conversion at the top of this page.
Depot
A tank site where Mount Meru holds oil, such as Beira. "Depot stock" shows each depot's balance, value, average cost and capacity.
Dollars per tonne (USD/MT)
The unit the CTRM uses for flat prices, costs and MTM. See the conversion at the top of this page.
Exposure
Tonnes whose futures price is still open, so they gain or lose with every market move. See Unpriced quantity and Unmatched exposure.
Fix lot
Locking the futures price on part of a period, usually 250 to 500 MT. You press "Fix lot" in the "Pricing workbench"; another trader then confirms it.
Futures MTM
The part of MTM that comes from the futures price moving. Example: 5,000 MT fixed at an average of 67.50, market 69.00: 5,000 × 1.50 × 22.0462 = +165,346.50 USD.
Headroom
How much more an LC can still cover: amount × (1 + tolerance) minus what is drawn. Example: a 7,000,000 USD LC with 10% tolerance and nothing drawn has 7,700,000 USD headroom.
LC
A letter of credit: a bank's promise to pay the seller against correct documents. An "LC at sight" pays on presentation; a "Usance LC" pays after a tenor such as 60 days.
Legal entity
One of the Mount Meru companies you trade through, such as MM-MZ. You see only records of the entities an Admin granted you.
Maker-checker
One person makes a change and a different person approves it. You never see "Approve" or "Confirm" on your own work.
Mark
Today's market value for futures or basis, used to value positions. A mark stays "pending" until finance or management approves it, never a trader.
Maturity
The date an accepted LC must be paid. It is the trigger date plus the tenor, moved past any holiday to the next business day. Example: B/L date plus 60 days.
MTM
Mark-to-market: the gain or loss from comparing your locked-in price with today's market price. For a purchase, MTM = quantity priced × (market price − average price). A sale flips the sign.
Nomination
A customer's request to collect a stated quantity from a depot by truck. Operations submits it to the port before the trucks load. See "Nominations".
Outturn
The quantity actually received at discharge. You type it as "Outturn" in "Mark discharged".
Over-pricing
More tonnes priced than the B/L delivered. Example: 5,000 MT priced, B/L 4,900 MT: 100 MT is over-priced, and moves 100 × 22.0462 = 2,204.62 USD per USc/lb.
Pricing window
The dates, about 30 days, in which you may fix lots for a period. "Pricing window start" and "Pricing window end" are set on the contract.
q_max
The most tonnes a period may be priced or shipped: the planned quantity plus the tolerance. Example: 5,000 MT at ±5% gives a q_max of 5,250 MT, so a 300 MT lot on top of 5,000 MT is refused.
Reference month
The ZL futures month a period prices against, as a month code such as N27 (July 2027). It is set on the contract's pricing term.
Roll
When the reference month expires, the daily MTM values the period against the next listed month. It records which month it used.
Sensitivity
How many USD a figure moves for each 1 USc/lb move in futures. Example: 2,000 MT unpriced × 22.0462 = 44,092.40 USD per USc/lb.
Snapshot
A stored picture of the figures for one date. The "Daily snapshot" is one entity's stock, MTM and exposure; an MTM snapshot is one period's value on one day. A snapshot is never changed, only replaced by a new version.
Stale
The MTM used an older approved mark because the day's mark was not approved before the run. It shows as "Stale" or "Stale mark". Approving the mark later reruns that day's MTM.
Tenor
The number of days between the trigger date (the B/L date or acceptance) and payment under an LC.
Tolerance
The allowed variation on a quantity, such as ±5% on a 5,000 MT period. On an LC, "Tolerance" is the extra percentage the LC covers.
Transit loss
The tonnes lost between loading and discharge: B/L quantity minus outturn. Example: 5,000 loaded, 4,990 discharged: 10 MT, 0.20%. "Transit loss" flags a loss above the product's tolerance.
TWAP
Time-weighted average price: the plain average of the daily settlements across a pricing window. It is the benchmark for the execution score.
Unmatched exposure
Unpriced purchase tonnes minus unpriced sale tonnes on the same futures month, within one entity. Example: 3,000 MT purchases and 1,200 MT sales unpriced: 1,800 MT, or 39,683.16 USD per USc/lb.
Unpriced quantity
Tonnes in a period whose futures price is not fixed yet: the period's reference quantity minus the confirmed lots.
VWAP
Volume-weighted average price: each lot's price weighted by its tonnes. The CTRM's "Average price" is a VWAP.
ZL
The CBOT ticker for soybean oil futures, quoted in USc/lb. On "Market marks" it is "ZL (CBOT soybean oil)".
