Chapter 0 · Read first · 10 minutes

The study plan

SSRD runs from Oct 19 to Nov 15 at about 10 hours a week. Learning can't take a whole week of that, so this plan gives each idea one evening, just before the week that needs it.

The SPEC lists the week 1 "learning evenings": CT and static CT, favicon mmh3, TLSH, urlscan syntax, precision and base rates, and Phillips & Wilder. Each one has a chapter here. Two more chapters (safe fetching and the VCU memo) cover things you are already doing or will do soon. Every chapter ends with questions. A chapter is done when you can answer them without looking.

Chapter 1 · Sun Oct 18 · 30 minutes

If they rebuild, would anyone see it?

On Oct 14 2026 the Brooklyn DA's Virtual Currency Unit said it had disrupted 241 domains and 112 cloud servers of a crypto investment scam aimed at Russian speakers. Its release also said "new scam websites popped up quickly." SSRD turns that sentence into a question you can measure: how fast do new sites that match the same kit appear, and can a passive observer see them?

What you'll learn
The victim funnel, where SSRD can and can't see it, and what a "kit" is.
Why it matters
Every limitation in the memo traces back to this chapter: cloaking, the CT-only view, a thin seed set.
In the repo
SPEC.md Goal, docs/research.md "The VCU case" and "Prior art".

1.1 The funnel, and the one step SSRD can see

The release describes a five-step funnel. Russian-language Facebook ads lead to a phone "advisor". The advisor steers the victim to a fake trading platform. When the victim tries to withdraw, they are charged "fees". Later comes a "recovery" scam that promises to get the money back. The sites referenced US agencies and Elon Musk to look legitimate.

1.2 A kit is software, not people

Fake investment sites are rarely built one by one. Operators reuse a kit: the same templates, scripts, icons and copy, re-skinned with a new brand name for each domain. The brand changes, but much of the software underneath stays the same. That reuse is the whole bet. If the software survives a rename, a fingerprint of it can link a brand-new domain to sites already known to be scams.

This is also why SSRD's language rules exist. A shared kit shows the same software. Kits are sold and copied, so it doesn't show the same operator. That is why the project always says "matches kit X" and never "operated by". The memo calls its findings "investigative leads, not attribution".
