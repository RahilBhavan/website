Stale marks and snapshots

Basis marks have no free public feed. A trader enters them from broker quotes, and Finance or Management approves them, not another trader. If the day's mark is not approved before the run, MTM uses the last approved mark and shows Stale. Approving later reruns that day. A snapshot is never recalculated in place. A correction writes a new version that supersedes the old one, and the snapshot stores the engine_version that made it.

The pricing engine is pure

backend/pricing/engine has no Django imports, no database, no outside calls. Inputs and outputs are plain dataclasses. A failing golden case blocks merge, and nobody edits a golden case to make a test pass. Changes need the analyst.

Chapter 6

Exposure and execution

After this chapter you can
  • Compute unmatched exposure and its sensitivity.
  • Say what spreading fixes (DCA) can and cannot do.
  • Compute a TWAP execution score.

The risk number that matters is not unpriced purchases alone. It is what is left after matching sales priced off the same reference.

Unmatched exposure (MT) = Σ unpriced purchase tonnes − Σ unpriced CBOT-linked sale tonnes
Sensitivity = unmatched exposure x 22.0462 USD per 1 USc/lb

Example (GT-12): 3,000 MT unpriced purchases and 1,200 MT unpriced sales leave 1,800 MT unmatched, which is 39,683.16 USD per USc/lb. The cheapest way to cut it is to fix purchase lots on the same days the matching sales are priced.

What spreading fixes buys

CBOT soybean oil is a deep market. Mount Meru's volume does not move it. No fixing schedule changes the price you should expect. A schedule changes how widely the result can vary.

Even spreading is the right default. It is a risk tool, not a way to buy cheaper.

Scoring a window: TWAP

The TWAP is the plain average of the window's daily settlements. The execution score compares your achieved average with it.

Result = s x (TWAP − F avg) x 22.0462 x Qpriced   positive = beat even spreading

GT-11: settlements 66.00, 67.00, 67.50, 68.00, 69.00 give TWAP 67.50. You fixed 5,000 MT at 67.30. Result = (67.50 − 67.30) x 22.0462 x 5,000 = +22,046.20 USD. You beat even spreading.

The system measures execution. It does not promise to predict prices. Over a season the scoreboard shows whether discretion adds value or only adds risk.

Chapter 7

Contracts and delivery periods

After this chapter you can
  • Explain why pricing hangs off the period, not the header.
  • Compute q_min, q_max and the derived quantities.

A contract is a header. The real behaviour lives on its delivery periods, because each month is priced, shipped and financed on its own.

The header carries

  • Direction (purchase or sale), legal entity, counterparty, product, trade date.
  • Incoterm (FOB, CFR, CIF, DAP, FCA). It decides who pays freight and insurance and where the price is marked.
  • Total quantity, tolerance, currency, payment instrument and terms (for example 60 days from the B/L date).
  • Maker and checker. They must be different people. Numbers look like MMP-2027-0001 (purchase) and MMS-2027-0001 (sale).

Derived values are never stored

They are computed when read, so they cannot drift.

q_min = planned x (1 − tol)   q_max = planned x (1 + tol)
q_priced = Σ confirmed lots   q_unpriced = max(q_reference − q_priced, 0)
q_reference = q_shipped once shipped, otherwise planned

For 5,000 MT at ±5%: q_min = 4,750 MT, q_max = 5,250 MT.

Pricing types

fixedA flat price at signing

Contract changes after approval are amendments. Each approved amendment increments version_no. Maker-checker approval applies to contracts, lots, marks, adjustments and access changes. Holding two roles never lets you approve your own work.
