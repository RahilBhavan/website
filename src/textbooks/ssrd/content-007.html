8.7 Time to flag: median and p90, not the mean

Time to flag runs from the CT timestamp to the first alert. A few sites flagged only on the +24 h retry would drag a mean far from the typical case. So report the median (half the alerts were faster) and p90 (90% were faster). Count only box uptime, and report gaps.

Check yourself

Base rate 2%, recall 50%, false-positive rate 1%, 10,000 pages. Precision?
TP = 10,000 × 0.02 × 0.5 = 100. FP = 9,800 × 0.01 = 98. Precision ≈ 100 / 198 ≈ 51%.
Why does a decoy count against precision even if it's probably a scam?
Precision claims what you confirmed. You didn't see the scam content, so the claim isn't supported. Reporting the decoy share separately keeps the information without inflating the headline.
You swept TLSH on the week 3 labels and then reported precision on those same labels. What's wrong, and how do you fix it?
The threshold was fitted to those labels' noise, so precision is optimistic. Freeze the threshold, then report precision only on alerts from the week 4 run, labeled afterwards.
Two labelers agree 90% of the time but κ = 0.2. How?
One label dominates. If almost everything is "not-scam", chance agreement pe is already near 0.9, so agreeing 90% adds little beyond luck.

Writing a lead an investigator can use

The memo goes to a prosecutor who leads the unit and a senior analyst. They're busy, they'll check your claims, and anything they act on may one day be read aloud in court. Two pages have to tell them what you found, how sure you are, and what you can't see.

What you'll learn
Conclusion-first structure, confidence labels, wording that never overclaims, and the process rules around sending.
Why it matters
It's the project's deliverable. An overclaim here undoes the care taken everywhere else.
In the repo

9.1 Conclusion first

Put the answer in the first three lines: how many new domains matched which kits, over what window, at what precision with its interval. Everything after supports that paragraph. A reader who stops after it should still have the result and its main limit. This is Barbara Minto's pyramid principle: the governing thought, then the reasons, then the evidence.

9.2 Every claim carries a label

LabelMeansExample
confirmedDirectly observed and re-checkable from your evidence."Domain A served a page whose copy shares 84 5-grams with kit-01-KCDA seed pages (capture SHA-256 in the appendix)."

The examples are illustrative wording, not findings.

9.4 A two-page skeleton

  1. Summary. The result with numbers and intervals, the window, and "investigative leads, not attribution".
  2. What the kits share. One sentence per kit, the signals that define it, KCDA kits separate from general CBR kits.
  3. What appeared, and when. New matches since the takedown, time from takedown to first match, and the time-to-flag median and p90.
  4. Method in five lines. CT → filter → passive visit → fingerprints → two-family rule → human review, with a link to the public repo.
  5. Leads. A table with the domain (defanged), kit, signals matched, confidence label and capture hash. Private, never in the repo.
  6. Limitations. Cloaking and the decoy share, filter recall (0.55 on future CBR names), wildcard certs, a CT-only view of a funnel that starts with Facebook ads, a thin KCDA seed set, and box downtime.

9.5 Process rules

  • Nothing is sent until Rahil approves the final text. Drafts, renders and this book are not approval.
  • Find the intake channel through the DA's office main line. Don't guess email addresses.
  • Send findings; don't offer to collect on request (chapter 7.3).
  • No operator names, no victim data, no addresses or handles in the body. Linking evidence stays in the private appendix.
