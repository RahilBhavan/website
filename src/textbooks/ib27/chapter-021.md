## Chapter 17 — Contribution and Exchange-Ratio Analysis

### Learning objectives

- Calculate contribution percentages.
- Compare negotiated ownership with economic lenses.
- Avoid mechanical fairness claims.

### Why it matters

Exchange ratios combine negotiation, market value, earnings, control, and strategic expectations.

### Explanation

For ACLS metric \(A\) and VECO metric \(V\):

\[
ACLS\ mix=A/(A+V)
\]

\[
VECO\ mix=V/(A+V)
\]

The project reports implied ratios approximately:

- Revenue: 0.3976x.
- Operating income: 0.5963x.
- Mid-cycle operating income: 0.8945x.
- EPS-neutral implied ratio, workbook framework: 0.1538x.

Market-cap contribution is a separate mix: **62.64% ACLS / 37.36% VECO**.

### Project connection

The final 0.3575 ratio is closer to revenue and market-cap lenses than to trough-year OI contribution, and it is reported within disclosed adviser fairness ranges. That comparison is an analyst judgment, not an independent fairness determination.

### Worked example — Illustration

If ACLS contributes $100 and VECO $60:

\[
VECO\ contribution=60/(100+60)=37.5\%
\]

### Common mistakes

- Using rounded percentages to reproduce exact ratios.
- Calling one contribution measure “fair value.”
- Confusing the EPS-neutral implied ratio with market-cap contribution.
- Ignoring negotiation and adviser fairness analyses.

### Checklist

- [ ] Use unrounded workbook values.
- [ ] Identify metric and period.
- [ ] Compare multiple lenses.
- [ ] Label judgment.
- [ ] Cite adviser materials when discussing fairness ranges.

### Takeaways

Contribution analysis frames the debate; it does not settle it.
