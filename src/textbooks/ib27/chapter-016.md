## Chapter 13 — Risk, Regulation, and Uncertainty

### Learning objectives

- Separate known conditions from unknown probabilities.
- Explain SAMR restraint.
- Build a risk matrix.

### Why it matters

False precision is especially dangerous in regulatory and merger-arbitrage analysis.

### Explanation

The frozen project reports SAMR as the remaining regulatory condition, **as stated in the applicable registered agreement and transaction materials**, but does not assign approval odds. The agreement materials identify an outside-date ladder:

- Initial date: 2026-09-30.
- First extension: 2027-03-30.
- Second extension: 2027-06-30.

### Project connection

The model uses assumed 50%, 60%, and 75% approval probabilities only as illustrative break-value scenarios. They are not findings.

### Worked example — Illustration

\[
Break=
\frac{VECO\ price-p\times Deal\ value}{1-p}
\]

At 60%, the workbook’s illustrative break value is approximately $61.46.

### Common mistakes

- Calling an assumption a probability estimate.
- Treating a spread as a floor.
- Ignoring outside dates and fee triggers.
- Presenting a frozen regulatory statement as current status.

### Checklist

- [ ] Report dated regulatory facts.
- [ ] Label probabilities as assumptions.
- [ ] State unknowns.
- [ ] Avoid approval forecasts without evidence.

### Takeaways

The absence of a defensible probability is itself a valid analytical conclusion.
