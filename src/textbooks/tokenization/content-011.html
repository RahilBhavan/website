Tokenomics checks

T1: no mechanical holder cash-flow increase. The voting token has no stated distribution rule. Investigate whether rights or demand mechanisms change and whether those are enforceable or merely possible.
T2: circulating cap $120 million; maximum-based FDV $600 million. An unlock changes float under the assumed methodology, not outstanding supply; minting adds outstanding units. The provider must explain exclusions, locked units and treatment of bridged representations.
T3: simplified residual $1 million. Its legal and economic owner depends on the structure. Evidence could include enforceable distribution terms, fee routing in deployed contracts, relevant legal documents and the authority to change these rules. Governance expectation is not an existing guaranteed payment.
T4: 10% balance growth. Share falls from 10% to 220/2,400 = 9.17%. Dollar value changes from $1,000 to $880, or -12%. Yield can change with subsidy, participation, fees, token price or protocol rules.

RWA checks

R1: an unsecured issuer claim has issuer-credit and enforcement exposure; it does not establish an allocated property interest or physical delivery entitlement. Read governing terms, custody and allocation records, security documentation if any, and the relevant legal analysis.
R2: initial residual $40,000. Rent falls to $270,000 and residual to $10,000, a 75% decline. Fixed obligations magnify changes in the residual. Principal repayment, taxes or unusual expenses would require more information.
R3: net assets $5 million; NAV $1.25 per unit; $1.20 is a 4% discount. Eligibility, fees, minimums, stale valuation, delays and suspension can prevent capturing it.
R4: cash shortfall $700,000. This does not alone prove insolvency. To raise $700,000 at 70% of book, sell assets with $1 million carrying value and recognize a $300,000 loss, under the simplified assumptions.
R5: senior receives $6 million, zero loss; junior receives $1 million and loses $3 million, or 75%. Backing does not specify asset value, custody, priority, liabilities or enforcement costs.
R6: the dashboard reports a token quantity or valuation, not independently established current backing. One dated report supports only its stated custodian, assets, date and scope. Obtain the other custodian's evidence, current liabilities, supply reconciliation and the relevant rights documents.

Tokenization checks

Z1: 1.25 asset units per share; deposit mints 200 shares; redemption returns 125 asset units. Actual fees, rounding, limits and available liquidity can alter execution. An interface is not a liquidity promise.
Z2: reuse the same operation ID; record payment confirmation and link a single issuance transaction; inspect pending or finalized transaction state before replacement or retry. Do not equate a network timeout with failed execution. Reconcile cash, register and chain after recovery.
Z3: no universal operation policy follows from "ineligible." A strong answer states terms and law assumptions, handles legitimate exits and mistakes, identifies authorized reviewers, and prevents bypass through allowances or contract recipients. Do not substitute arbitrary freezing for a policy.
Z4: the cash and asset legs are separate across time and systems. One party may deliver first and bear counterparty exposure. Prove any guaranteed linkage with the actual mechanism, intermediaries, finality rules and legal treatment.
Z5: duplicate inputs create one issuance; stale NAV prevents affected issuance under the stated policy; unauthorized mint fails; signer loss follows tested quorum recovery; pause behavior matches terms; restart resumes or reconciles one issuance. Observe registers and cash/token balances, not just a successful response.
Z6: possible benefits include shared servicing or integration needs; sparse transfers weaken a settlement-volume justification. Stop if measured lifecycle cost, risks or investor experience do not improve enough to justify the change. The burden is to establish a useful result, not to deploy a token.

Building expertise after the first pass

First complete all three loop capstones with disclosed assistance. Then select three contrasting real systems: a native network token, a tokenized financial claim and a tokenization platform. Investigate each with all three lenses, using actual documents and reproducible calculations. Do not reuse a marketing taxonomy as your analysis.

Practice professional disagreement. Write a conclusion, the strongest objection, what evidence would change your mind and what remains unknown. Ask a reviewer to challenge the legal claim, economic model or operational recovery separately. A good analysis narrows uncertainty; it does not conceal it behind a score.

For a second pass, deepen five areas: monetary economics and mechanism design; accounting and corporate finance; fixed income and credit underwriting; securities and property-law structure in a chosen jurisdiction; smart-contract and distributed-system security. Each area is a discipline of its own. This book supplies a foundation and an assessment path, not exhaustive professional training in all five.

An expert-level working standard is the ability to investigate an unfamiliar product, reconcile its claims, build a model, find failure modes, defend uncertainty and update a conclusion when evidence changes. Your current readiness remains unassessed until you complete independent work.

Glossary

Allowance: permission for a spender to transfer tokens up to a specified amount under a token's rules.
Attestation: an assurance statement whose meaning depends on scope, date and standard.
Atomicity: all-or-nothing completion within a specified execution domain.
Backing: a claimed economic or legal relationship requiring asset, custody, liability and enforcement evidence.
Burn: removal of units from supply under the relevant rules.
Collateral: assets pledged or used to support an obligation, subject to an enforceable arrangement.
Custodian: a party responsible for holding or safeguarding specified assets under defined terms.
Dilution: reduction in a holder's relative claim or share, often through additional issuance.
Duration: a measure related to cash-flow timing and bond interest-rate sensitivity.
FDV: token price multiplied by a stated future or maximum supply assumption.
Finality: the point at which a transaction is treated as irreversible under specified technical or legal rules.
Float: units practically available for trading under a stated definition.
Governance: rules and powers for deciding or changing system behavior.
Haircut: reduction applied to a valuation, collateral amount or recovery.
Invariant: a property required to hold across all valid system transitions.
Liquidity: ability to trade or meet obligations on acceptable terms and timing.
Mint: creation of units under authorized rules.
NAV: assets minus liabilities under a defined valuation policy; NAV per unit divides by relevant units.
Oracle: a mechanism supplying external information to a system.
Primary market: issuance and redemption interactions with the issuer or authorized counterparties.
Redemption: exchange or cancellation of a claim for value according to its terms.
Secondary market: transfers or trades of already-issued instruments between holders.
Servicer: a party administering assets, collections, reporting or recoveries.
Slashing: protocol-imposed loss of staked capital for defined misconduct in systems that implement it.
Solvency: ability of assets to cover liabilities under specified valuation and legal assumptions.
SPV: special-purpose vehicle; its name alone does not establish asset segregation or insolvency protection.
Spread: difference between buy and sell quotes, or between yields, depending on context.
Tranche: a class of claims with specified payment or loss priority.
TVL: total value locked under a particular methodology; not automatically revenue, unique capital or recoverable collateral.
Vesting: a schedule governing acquisition or release of rights to units.
Waterfall: ordered allocation of cash or recovery among claims.
