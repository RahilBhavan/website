Why a token exists

After this chapter: Explain how service demand can differ from token-holder demand.

Watch for this mistake: A useful product automatically makes its governance token valuable.

Start with the service. Who has a problem? What do they pay for? What scarce resource delivers it? Then identify the token's job. Payment, collateral, security bonding, governance and legal cash-flow rights are different jobs. A system can have users without giving its governance token a claim on fees.

Imagine a file-storage network. Customers want reliable storage; operators supply disks and bandwidth. A bond can discourage misconduct if wrongdoing is observable and punishable. But requiring customers to acquire a volatile token adds friction. A designer must explain why ordinary currency, reputation or contractual deposits cannot achieve the same purpose more simply.

Map each participant's payoff. A user values service minus price and effort. An operator values rewards minus hardware, capital and penalties. A founder values long-run adoption but may face an early unlock. A voter may hold another position that benefits from a harmful proposal. Economic analysis asks what participants can gain by deviating, not whether the documentation says they are aligned.

Network usage and token demand are separate. Fees collected in dollars may accrue entirely to operators. A rapidly reused payment token may support high transaction volume with small balances. The identity M x V = P x Q can organize a monetary model, but its variables and time units must be defined, and it is not a reliable token-price forecast. Velocity, speculation and endogenous demand complicate causal interpretation.

Attempt: list the token's job in a fictional ride-sharing app. Worked reasoning: loyalty points may be useful, but a freely traded governance token is not automatically needed to dispatch rides. An alternative database is a serious baseline. Fresh check T1: a computation network accepts stablecoins and pays operators directly. Its separate token only votes on parameters. Does doubling revenue mechanically double the token's cash flows? Explain the missing mechanism.

Read: ERC-20 [1] for what a common token interface actually provides. It supplies balance, transfer and approval operations, not an economic business model.

Supply, distribution and dilution

After this chapter: Reconcile outstanding supply and distinguish minting from unlocking.

Watch for this mistake: An unlock always creates new outstanding units.

Track four quantities: outstanding supply, circulating supply under an explicit methodology, maximum or modeled future supply, and immediately sellable float. "Circulating" can differ across data providers. A locked token can be outstanding without being sellable. A bridge can lock an original and mint a representation; adding both as independent economic units can double-count claims.

A supply reconciliation is stronger than a pie chart. Beginning outstanding + newly minted - destroyed = ending outstanding. Unlocking existing units changes availability, not necessarily total supply. Vesting grants rights over time; a cliff delays the first release. Check the actual vesting contract and any administrator's ability to change it.

Worked example: a token trades at $2. There are 100 million circulating units and a modeled maximum of 1 billion. Circulating market cap is $200 million; fully diluted valuation using that maximum is $2 billion. Neither number is the amount of cash invested, nor the proceeds obtainable by selling every unit at the last price.

A holder owns 1 million of 100 million outstanding units: 1%. If 25 million are newly issued and the holder receives none, ownership falls to 0.8%. That is a 20% relative reduction in ownership share, although supply grew 25%. If instead 25 million previously outstanding locked units unlock, your share of total outstanding is unchanged while market float rises.

Study distribution by effective control: beneficial owners, treasury signers, delegates, custodians, exchange omnibus accounts and affiliated wallets. Address count is not person count. A beautiful vesting schedule cannot prevent hidden hedges or guarantee recipients will sell gradually.
