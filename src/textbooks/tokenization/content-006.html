RWA due diligence as an investigation

After this chapter: Match each backing claim with appropriate dated evidence.

Watch for this mistake: Visible token supply plus a reserve statement proves complete net backing.

Use an evidence hierarchy appropriate to the question. Governing documents establish promised rights. Asset statements, independent reports and reconciliation support existence and valuation. On-chain state supports supply and control observations. Marketing can identify a claim to investigate but cannot verify itself.

Build the cash trail from borrower or asset through collection, custody, fees, taxes, reserves and investor payout. Build the failure trail from missed payment through reporting, grace periods, default, enforcement, asset sale and recovery distribution. A design that describes only good times is incomplete.

Compare to the non-tokenized alternative using the same asset exposure. Measure subscription time, reconciliation effort, settlement failures, total fees, investor access and recovery process. Fractional ownership may lower ticket size while eligibility restrictions still exclude many investors. Programmable transfer rules may reduce some manual checks while creating administrator and software dependencies.

Three study cases: (A) a short-duration government-debt fund: inspect holdings, share rights, dealing windows and cash rails; (B) private credit: inspect borrower concentration, underwriting, covenants, valuation and workouts; (C) property: inspect title, leverage, operating cash, local law and exit options. These are categories for investigation, not products endorsed by this book.

Fresh check R6: a dashboard shows $20 million in tokens, but the latest independent asset report is three months old and covers only one of two custodians. State exactly what is known, unknown and needed next. Do not claim fraud or full backing from incomplete evidence.

Real-world assets: deeper investigations

Securitization and tokenization solve different problems

Securitization structures cash-flow claims from a pool of assets, often using a vehicle and different classes of claims. Tokenization changes how a claim is represented and operated. A securitization can use conventional records; a tokenized claim can exist without tranches.

Imagine a pool of invoices. Borrowers pay the vehicle. Servicing costs are paid according to the documents. Senior investors receive their specified amounts before junior investors receive residual cash. Digitizing both classes does not change their priority unless the legal structure changes too. A smart contract may automate available-cash distribution while the collection and enforcement process remains external.

Separate defaults from losses. A borrower may default but later repay in full; it may pay principal late with substantial enforcement costs. A pool's average expected loss can hide concentration. Ten loans to businesses dependent on one customer may not provide ten independent exposures.

Try it: two portfolios have identical average borrower default probability. One contains 100 independent small loans; the other contains 100 loans exposed to the same commodity price. Why can their expected loss be similar while severe-loss risk differs? State what evidence would establish concentration.

Duration, credit spreads and the illusion of a stable screen

A bond's required yield can be decomposed analytically into a benchmark rate plus a credit or liquidity spread, although the decomposition depends on the instrument and model. A price can fall because rates rise, credit worsens or market liquidity deteriorates. Tokenization does not erase these drivers.

Modified duration gives a local approximation: percentage price change ≈ minus duration × yield change. If duration is 4 and required yield rises 0.02, estimated price change is -8%. This approximation omits convexity and other changes. It works best for sufficiently small yield movements under its assumptions.

A displayed NAV can appear stable because prices update slowly. That stability can be measurement latency rather than low economic risk. Compare market quotes, valuation frequency, model inputs and the liquidation price realistically obtainable. Thinly traded loans can show smooth marks until a default forces a large revision.

Try it: an instrument has modified duration 2.5 and yield rises 1.2 percentage points. Estimate the local price effect. Then explain why that estimate cannot predict the actual recovery on a defaulted loan.

Redemption design determines who absorbs a run

In an open-ended vehicle, investors may request cash before all assets mature. If early redeemers receive an overstated NAV and the vehicle sells assets at a loss, remaining investors can bear a disproportionate burden. The price, timing and cost-allocation rules influence incentives to redeem first.

Suppose a fund has $10 million stated assets and 10 million shares. Economic asset value is actually $9 million, but stale NAV still says $1. If two million shares redeem at $1 with no other changes, $7 million remains for eight million shares: $0.875 each. Redeeming at an accurately measured $0.90 would leave $7.2 million for eight million shares, preserving $0.90. This illustration isolates stale valuation and ignores other costs.

Gates, notice periods, liquidity buffers and anti-dilution mechanisms address different parts of this problem. None is a universal solution. A gate can protect asset value while limiting access to cash; a buffer can meet withdrawals while reducing invested assets. Check what the actual documents authorize and who decides.

Try it: repeat the stale-NAV calculation with one million redemptions. Identify the loss borne by exiting versus remaining holders under both pricing rules.

Reconcile existence, ownership and encumbrance separately

Evidence that an asset exists does not prove that it belongs to the issuing vehicle. Evidence of ownership does not prove it is free of liens or pledged elsewhere. Evidence of unencumbered assets does not reveal all liabilities. Net backing requires several independently supported propositions.

Write four columns: asset existence, legal ownership, encumbrances, and obligations. A custodian statement may support some columns but not all. A blockchain proof may support a token balance while leaving the relationship between that token and a physical asset unresolved. Read assurance reports for exactly what they tested, at which date and with what exclusions.

Use aligned observation times. A report of assets on Monday and supply on Friday can miss intervening minting, borrowing or asset transfers. Frequent publication helps only when the measurements are meaningful, complete and reconciled. More dashboard refreshes do not make a narrow attestation comprehensive.

Try it: write a cautious conclusion for a vehicle with verified assets of $12 million, reported tokens of $10 million and no independent liabilities evidence. Explain why “120% fully backed” is not yet established.

Separate contracts, ledgers and courts in a default

An RWA default can cross several domains. The contract may mark a payment overdue. A servicer may contact the borrower. A trustee or security agent may enforce rights. A court may stay action or determine priority. Cash recovered later must be reconciled and distributed.

The right to accelerate a loan, seize collateral or appoint a receiver comes from the relevant law and documents. A code transition named “default” does not itself create those rights. Conversely, a court-recognized right may still require operational action before a token holder receives value.

Build a recovery timeline with uncertain durations and costs. Include notification, cure period, acceleration, enforcement, sale, legal expenses and distribution. Distinguish promised procedure from observed performance. Ask whether a servicer can be replaced and who holds the data and account access needed to continue collections.

Try it: the original platform disappears while the vehicle still owns performing loans. Identify the records, authority, keys and bank access a replacement operator would need. This is an operational continuity problem as well as a legal one.

A tokenized product has two cash problems

The first cash problem is investment liquidity: can assets be sold or collected to fund an exit? The second is payment-rail liquidity: is usable cash in the correct account or settlement asset at the required time? A vehicle can have enough total cash and still miss payment because the money is in another currency, bank or unavailable rail.

A stablecoin-funded subscription introduces another claim in the chain. The vehicle must decide whether to hold it, redeem it or convert it, and who bears depeg, conversion and settlement risk. “Dollars on-chain” is not a sufficient accounting description. Define the unit of account and the actual asset received.

Try it: a vehicle owes $500,000 in bank dollars today but holds $500,000 face value of a token whose issuer redeems only for approved institutions on business days. What facts determine whether the obligation can be met? Separate a secondary sale from primary redemption and account for fees.

Learning loop 03

Tokenization

How does a claim move through issuance, transfer, settlement and recovery?

Outcome and entry test

You will design and challenge an end-to-end asset lifecycle, including legal linkage, ledger state, identity, cash settlement and recovery. Tokenization is the process of representing and managing an asset or right through tokens and the surrounding system. It can apply to native digital assets as well as off-chain claims; RWA is a major application rather than a synonym.

Entry test: describe every step required to turn a $100 investment in a fund into transferable digital shares and later return cash. Mark which steps require code, a financial institution, legal documents or human decisions. Revisit this after Chapter 18.

Prerequisites: ownership versus key control, assets versus liabilities, and a ledger as a record of state. This loop does not require a live deployment, wallet funding or real asset issuance.
