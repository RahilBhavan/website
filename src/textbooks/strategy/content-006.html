Chapter 15

2025–2026: the cost of defending the credit

Once the premium shrank, the question changed from "how fast can we grow?" to "how do we keep the preferreds trusted?" Every tool for that costs something. Here's what.

The USD Reserve

The trade. Keep $4.88B in dollars instead of bitcoin.

What it costs. That's about 59,400 BTC Strategy could have owned. If bitcoin rises 30%, the Reserve has forgone about $1.46B of gains. Treasury-bill interest offsets part of that cost.

What it buys. Insurance. With 37 months of charges in cash, Strategy doesn't have to sell stock below net assets or sell bitcoin in a downturn. Insurance is worth most exactly when bitcoin falls, which is when the forgone-gains cost is negative.

Buying back STRC

What it costs. Dilution: common holders sold part of the company to retire a claim. And bitcoin buying slowed, because the money went to buybacks instead.

Why it still added value. MSTR was the expensive security (above 1.0x) and STRC the cheap one (below $100). Selling the expensive security to retire the cheap one adds value until STRC trades above $100 × mNAV, about $119 in October.

Daily dividends

The yield math. A 12% rate paid twice a month and reinvested earns about 12.72% a year. Paid daily, about 12.75%. The difference is only 0.03 points, so daily payment isn't really about yield.

The real economics. On each record date, a twice-monthly STRC drops by about $0.50 as the dividend comes off. Daily, the drop is about 3 cents. A smoother price makes STRC more like a cash account. It also removes the incentive to buy just before a record date and sell just after. Strategy's proxy lists price stability and easier access to preferred capital among its reasons. The cash cost to Strategy is unchanged, apart from running the payments.

Redefining mNAV and the 1.0x line

The economics. The definition matters because it decides when selling stock is "accretive". The net version subtracts every senior claim first, which matches the real question: does a new share bring in more net bitcoin than it takes? Measured that way, selling stock adds value above 1.0x and destroys it below. The change was criticized for its timing, but the net definition is the economically correct test for a company with large preferred claims.

The BTC Monetization Program

The economics. Below 1.0x mNAV, raising $100M by selling stock gives away about $125M of net assets at 0.8x. Selling $100M of bitcoin gives away $100M, plus any tax on gains and the price impact of selling. So below the line, selling bitcoin is the cheaper source of cash, which is exactly when the program allows it. The cost is the thing Strategy exists to avoid: owning fewer coins.

Chapter 16

Every lever in a squeeze, priced

Suppose Strategy needs to pay $100M of dividends. It has six ways to do it. Each one costs common holders a different amount, and the cheapest one depends on mNAV.

What the table says

  • Above 1.0x, stock is the cheapest source. That's why Strategy funds dividends with ATM sales whenever the premium allows, and why paying STRK in shares beats paying it in cash at 1.19x.
  • Below 1.0x, cash and bitcoin are cheaper than stock. That's the logic behind both the Reserve and the BTC Monetization Program.
  • Preferred issuance and skipping only defer the bill. They look free today and cost the most later.
  • The Reserve is neutral today but buys time. Its real value is that it lets Strategy wait for a better mNAV instead of selling stock or bitcoin at a bad moment.
The single rule behind every decisionRaise money from whichever source is most overpriced, and retire whichever claim is most underpriced. Stock when the premium is high. Bitcoin or cash when it's low. Buy back preferreds below $100, sell them above. Fourteen decisions in Chapter 5 are this rule applied as markets changed.

Check yourself

At 1.3x net mNAV, what does paying $100M in dividends by selling stock cost common holders?

About $77M of net assets ($100M ÷ 1.3), less than paying from cash.

Why isn't selling new preferred "free" even though it costs $0 of net assets today?

It creates a new claim (more than $100M if sold below $100) and a dividend paid every year forever. The cost is real, only postponed.

Chapter 17

Cash plumbing: where the dividend dollars come from

Bitcoin pays nothing, so every dividend dollar has to come from somewhere else. Strategy has built four pipes for this, plus two programs that send money back out.

What an ATM is

An at-the-market program lets a company sell shares into the market a little at a time through brokers, instead of one big offering. Strategy reports every week how much it sold and how much registered capacity is left. Capacity is a ceiling, not demand: it only turns into cash if buyers show up at a price worth selling at.

The order of defence

  1. Sell MSTR while it trades above its net bitcoin. That pays dividends without touching the Reserve.
  2. Spend the Reserve. About 37 months of charges today, 25 months above the policy floor.
  3. Pay STRK in shares to save about $112M a year, or skip STRD, which is legal but would damage trust.
  4. Sell bitcoin. Management has called this a last resort. The program allows up to $1.25B into the Reserve, and more with board approval.
Why a 12-month floor mattersIt turns "we have cash" into a rule investors can check every week. It also means the Reserve can't all be spent: only the part above 12 months of charges (≈ $3.3B today) is truly free before the board has to step in.

Chapter 18

Trading prices, tax and accounting

A dividend rate is different from a market yield

The rate applies to the stated amount. Current yield divides the annual dividend by the traded price. For a fixed 10% rate on $100 stated amount, the annual dividend is $10. At an illustrative $80 price, current yield is 12.5%; at $110, it is 9.09%. These are illustrations, not quotes.

A higher current yield can reflect credit concerns, liquidity, interest rates or structural differences. It does not measure expected total return. A perpetual preferred has no scheduled maturity at which the investor is assured $100. STRK’s conversion feature, STRC’s variable rate, STRE’s euro payments and the seniority ladder all affect comparisons.

Use the same quote time and currency for all five products. The workbook preserves source dates; it does not claim a refreshed executable quote. Dividend yield is not a yield to maturity or a guarantee of payment.

Return of capital still consumes cash

Strategy reported that its 2025 preferred distributions were return of capital for U.S. federal tax purposes to the extent of each holder’s basis. That reduces basis; distributions above basis are capital gains. The company’s expectation for future treatment depends on tax earnings and profits and can change. Tax classification does not reduce the issuer’s cash dividend obligation.

February results and tax explanation; Forms 8937 and issuer tax records. These describe issuer reporting, not a personalized tax conclusion.

Fair-value profit does not pay a dividend

Strategy adopted ASU 2023-08 in 2025. Changes in bitcoin fair value enter reported net income. An unrealized gain raises accounting earnings without producing cash. A fair-value loss can be recorded while the company still holds a cash reserve. The coverage model therefore separates cash charges, available liquidity and bitcoin value.

Q2 filing, digital-assets accounting policy. A bitcoin sale can create cash, with taxes and execution costs that the snapshot calculator does not estimate.

The software business remains an operating business

Q2 2026 revenue was $122.4M and gross profit was $81.6M. For scale only, four times that quarter’s revenue is $489.6M, about 31% of the model’s $1,559.7M annual cash charges. Revenue is before operating expenses, taxes, reinvestment and working capital, so this comparison is not operating cash coverage. The model assumes no software cash contribution until a source-backed cash forecast is added.
