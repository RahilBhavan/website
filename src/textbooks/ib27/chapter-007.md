## Chapter 5 — Accounting Foundations for Bankers

### Learning objectives

- Connect the three statements.
- Calculate margins, EV, EBITDA, and EPS.
- Identify what the project does not model.

### Why it matters

M&A analysis is accounting translated into ownership and earnings.

### Explanation

Core identities:

\[
GP=Revenue-COGS
\]

\[
OI=GP-R\&D-SG\&A
\]

\[
NI=Pre-tax\ income-Taxes
\]

\[
Assets=Liabilities+Equity
\]

\[
\Delta Cash=CFO+CFI+CFF
\]

\[
EV=Equity\ Value+Debt-Cash+Other\ Adjustments
\]

Some illustrative schedules use a clearly labeled **25% tax convention**. The scope definition sheet marks the effective tax rate as **pending**, so 25% is not a general filed fact or resolved project-wide tax conclusion.

### Project connection

The workbook separates operating schedules, transaction mechanics, convert treatment, and accretion. It does not claim a full working-capital, cash-flow, balance-sheet, or purchase-accounting model.

### Worked example — Illustration

Revenue $800 million, gross profit $320 million, operating expenses $250 million, D&A $20 million, debt $400 million, cash $100 million, equity value $1,200 million:

- Gross margin: 40%.
- Operating income/EBIT: $70 million.
- EBITDA: $90 million.
- EV: $1,500 million.

### Common mistakes

- Calling EBITDA cash flow.
- Mixing market capitalization and EV.
- Applying tax mechanically to unusual losses.
- Hiding convert treatment.
- Treating the 25% convention as the resolved effective tax rate.

### Checklist

- [ ] Periods match.
- [ ] Margins use matching denominators.
- [ ] EV bridge is explicit.
- [ ] Tax is labeled.
- [ ] Cash-flow limitations are stated.

### Takeaways

Accounting definitions must remain stable across historicals, forecasts, and merger schedules.
