Three tracks, one contract

After this chapter you can
  • Explain why pricing, physical and payment progress separately.
  • List the main records and who owns each.

A purchase contract can be half priced, on a ship, and financed by an accepted LC at the same time. No one status word describes that. So the system keeps three tracks.

Different measures, different names

Planned quantity, confirmed fixed quantity, pending requests, B/L quantity and outturn are not the same number. Compare like with like. A missing valuation is not a zero valuation.

Check yourself

  1. A period shows 3 of 5 lots confirmed, the vessel in transit, and an LC issued. Is it 'half done'?

    Answer

    No. Each track is at its own place. Read them separately.

  2. Does a requested lot count as priced?

    Answer

    No. Only a confirmed lot counts. Awaiting-confirmation tonnes stay in the unpriced tonnes.

Chapter 3

Units and prices

After this chapter you can
  • Convert USc/lb to USD/MT and back without a calculator trick.
  • Compute a lot's flat price from futures and basis.
  • Explain why the code converts in exactly one place.

CBOT soybean oil futures (ticker ZL) are quoted in US cents per pound. The CTRM works in US dollars per metric tonne. One tonne is 2,204.62 lb, so:

USD/MT = USc/lb x 2,204.62 / 100 = USc/lb x 22.0462

Flat price of a lot

Flat price (P) is futures (F) plus basis (B). Basis is the premium or discount the seller adds to the CBOT price. Positive is a premium.

P (USD/MT) = (F + B) x 22.0462   when F and B are both in USc/lb
P (USD/MT) = F x 22.0462 + B   when B is quoted in USD/MT

Reference month

Each lot prices against a named futures month. ZL months are F, H, K, N, Q, U, V, Z (Jan, Mar, May, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct, Dec). A shipment loading in May would normally use N (July), because K expires during May. The engine never guesses the month. The trader enters it on the pricing term and the system checks it against the list.

Money rules, from the project

Decimal only, never float. Quantities and money travel over the API as strings. Unit conversions live in one module, core/units.py. No ad-hoc * 1000 anywhere else. The browser does no pricing maths; the lot form shows the server's preview.

Where it lives backend/core/units.py backend/core/money.py backend/core/quantity.py

Check yourself

  1. Basis +3.50 USc/lb equals how many USD/MT?

    Answer

    3.50 x 22.0462 = 77.1617 USD/MT.

  2. A lot fixes at 66.00 USc/lb with basis +2.00. What is its flat price?

    Answer

    (66.00 + 2.00) x 22.0462 = 1,499.1416 USD/MT.

  3. Why not just use floats?

    Answer

    Floats cannot hold most decimal prices exactly. Small errors would break the rule that the futures and basis parts add up to the total, to the cent.

Chapter 4

Pricing lots and the average

After this chapter you can
  • Explain why a price is fixed in lots over a window.
  • Compute VWAP and the average price of a period.
  • Say how a lot is confirmed, rejected and reversed.

The buyer does not fix the whole cargo on one day. A pricing window (about 30 days) lets the trader lock the futures price in lots, usually 250 to 500 MT each. The average price is the volume-weighted average (VWAP) of the confirmed lots.

F avg = Σ(q × F) / Σq    P avg = Σ(q × P lot) / Σq

The engine always uses the general form on the right. It then works when the basis differs by lot.
