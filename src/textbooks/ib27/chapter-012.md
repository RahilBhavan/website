## Chapter 9 — ACLS and Ion Implantation

### Learning objectives

- Explain ACLS’s disclosed product category.
- Identify geographic and customer-concentration risks.
- Avoid unsupported competitive claims.

### Why it matters

A strategic thesis must be grounded in actual disclosed capabilities.

### Explanation

ACLS supplies ion-implantation systems. The registered ACLS filing identified in `research/01_sources/source_register.csv` states, as of its filing date, that ACLS and Applied Materials were the only suppliers described as offering a full range of ion-implant products. This is a source-bounded filing claim, not a timeless market fact.

ACLS’s 2025 data show:

- 83.7% of total revenue from international operations.
- 76.0% of ion-implanter system revenue from Asian customers.
- Top ten customers at 55.2% of net sales.
- One customer at 11.0%.

### Project connection

ACLS’s stronger current operating contribution helps explain the ownership debate, but current-cycle profitability is not the only valuation lens.

### Worked example — Illustration

If ACLS contributes $100 million of OI and VECO contributes $30 million:

\[
ACLS\ OI\ mix=100/(100+30)=76.92\%
\]

### Common mistakes

- Claiming ACLS wins every advanced-node opportunity.
- Ignoring Asian exposure.
- Treating “full range” as monopoly power.
- Removing the filing date from the competitive statement.

### Checklist

- [ ] Use disclosed product language.
- [ ] Cite the registered filing and date.
- [ ] Mention geographic exposure.
- [ ] Mention customer concentration.
- [ ] Label strategic interpretation.

### Takeaways

ACLS brings implant breadth, but the evidence does not prove node-specific dominance or immunity from cyclicality.
