Chapter 23

Three futures, quarter by quarter

Numbers stick better as stories. Here are three five-year paths for Strategy, each told the way a treasurer would live through it. The figures come from the coverage model's 20-quarter path (Chapter 27).

These are model runs on stated assumptions, not forecasts. Every path pays the full $6.7B of convertible puts in cash when they fall due, which is the harshest assumption.

Path 1: Base. The machine runs

Years 1–2. MSTR trades above its net assets, so Strategy keeps selling a little stock every day. The proceeds pay most of the ~$390M quarterly dividends and interest, so the Reserve barely moves. STRC trades near $100, and the rate holds at 12%.

Years 2–3. The convertible puts arrive: about $1.0B in Sep 2027, then $2.0B, $1.5B and $1.4B through 2028. Each one is a large one-time cash need, paid from stock sales and the Reserve.

Year 5. About $11.1B of common has been sold in total, every put has been paid, and $1.5B of cash is left. The cost is dilution: common holders now share the company with many more shares, though each share still holds a similar amount of bitcoin as long as the stock sold above net assets.

Path 2: The market shuts

Prices are unchanged, but suppose nobody will buy MSTR, or the stock trades below its net assets so selling it would cut bitcoin per share. The Reserve pays the dividends for about three years. The puts arrive in the middle of that. By the end of the path, the model is $9.5B short. To close that gap, Strategy has four real options: sell bitcoin (allowed into the Reserve up to $1.25B, more with board approval), pay STRK in shares, sell preferreds if they trade at $100, or negotiate with convertible holders.

Path 3: Severe

Quarter 1. Bitcoin falls 70% to about $24,600. Bitcoin's cover over the debt and preferreds drops to about 1.0x. MSTR trades below its net assets, so stock sales stop.

Quarters 1–4. STRC's price falls and its rate is pushed to 15%. Charges rise to about $1.83B a year. The Reserve now lasts about 32 months instead of 37.

Years 2–3. The Reserve runs down while puts fall due. In this scenario MSTR is far below the conversion prices, so holders will want cash. The gap reaches $10.8B over the path.

The choice at the end. Selling bitcoin at the bottom, the thing Strategy exists not to do, or leaning on the preferred terms: skipping STRD (non-cumulative), paying STRK in shares, and only then the senior layers, where STRF's and STRE's step-ups make skipping very expensive.

What the three paths teachThe near term is safe in all three: the Reserve covers about three years. The danger is the 2027–28 put window meeting a closed equity market. That's the date to watch, and it's why the coverage model tracks puts separately from dividends.

Check yourself

In the Base path, why doesn't the Reserve shrink much?

Daily stock sales above net assets pay most of the dividends, so the Reserve is rarely drawn on.

What's the first lever Strategy would likely pull in Path 3, and why that one?

Probably bitcoin sales into the Reserve or paying STRK in shares. Skipping STRD is legal but damages trust in every other preferred, and skipping STRF or STRE triggers expensive step-ups.

Chapter 24

Who decides

Many of the most important numbers in this book are choices, not market outcomes: STRC's rate, whether to sell stock this week, whether to skip a dividend, whether to sell bitcoin. So it matters who makes them.

The people

Michael Saylor co-founded MicroStrategy in 1989, led the move into bitcoin in 2020 and has been Executive Chairman since August 2022. He is also its public voice. Phong Le has been CEO since 2022, after serving as CFO when the strategy began.

Control

Saylor holds Class B shares that carry extra votes per share, giving him roughly a third of the total voting power (Oct 2026 proxy, as reported). That's not a majority, but it means a shareholder vote such as the Oct 28 daily-dividend amendment needs only modest support from other holders to pass.

Why this matters for the analysis

  • Discretion is a risk and a tool. A board that can adjust STRC's rate, pay STRK in shares or pause stock sales can respond to stress. Investors can't predict exactly how it will.
  • Key-person risk. The strategy, the brand and much of the investor following are tied to one person.
  • Aligned incentives. Saylor is a large holder of the common stock, which absorbs losses first. That aligns him with common holders, which isn't the same as aligning him with preferred holders.

Outside referees

Three outside parties shape Strategy's room to move. The SEC requires filings, including the weekly 8-Ks this book relies on. Rating agencies such as S&P (B-, Oct 2025) affect who may buy the securities. Index providers such as MSCI decide whether passive funds must hold MSTR, and froze its index share count growth in Jan 2026.

Check yourself

Who decides STRC's rate, and what limits them?

The board, on management's recommendation. They follow a published framework tied to STRC's trading price, and the current policy holds 12% until it trades near $100.

Chapter 25

Comparing treasury companies

Apply the same questions to each firm: what assets does it hold, what cash can those assets generate, what claims rank ahead of common, and what funding is available when issuance slows? Using Strategy’s net-coin definition across firms creates a comparison, not an endorsement of that definition.

How to make the comparison fair

Match observation dates, currencies and dilution definitions. Deduct senior claims once. Do not apply a staking yield to the whole treasury if only some ETH is staked; do not treat option premiums as risk-free recurring earnings. Compare net cash after costs with cash charges, and show principal deadlines separately.

The dat-accretion project asks a different question: whether a capital rotation changes net coins per share at disclosed prices. A rotation can add net coins per share while cash coverage weakens. Both measures are needed to describe the tradeoff.
