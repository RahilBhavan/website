## Chapter 25 — Investment Memo Structure

### Learning objectives

- Structure a decision-oriented memo.
- Put the conclusion before the history.
- Use falsifiers and limitations.

### Why it matters

A memo is not a data dump. It is a decision document.

### Explanation

A concise memo structure:

1. Executive conclusion.
2. Transaction terms.
3. Strategic rationale.
4. Contribution and valuation.
5. Accretion and synergy sensitivity.
6. Risks and regulatory status.
7. Falsifiers.
8. Appendix and sources.

### Project connection

The memo should state that the 0.3575 ratio **appears within the disclosed adviser fairness ranges identified in the relevant 424B3 annexes** and is reasonably positioned relative to selected contribution lenses. This is an analyst judgment, not an independent fairness determination. The economics remain dependent on recovery, the annual run-rate synergy guide, PPA, financing, costs-to-achieve, diluted shares, and regulatory timing.

### Worked example — Analyst judgment

> The fixed ratio appears within the disclosed adviser fairness ranges and is reasonably positioned relative to revenue and market-cap contribution. However, the base operating-proxy case is conditional because no-synergy results are dilutive and PPA, financing, costs-to-achieve, and full diluted-share treatment remain incomplete.

### Common mistakes

- Leading with methodology.
- Hiding the no-synergy case.
- Claiming current facts beyond the registered freeze.
- Omitting falsifiers.
- Making an independent fairness determination from adviser analyses.

### Checklist

- [ ] Conclusion stands alone.
- [ ] Facts and judgments labeled.
- [ ] At least three falsifiers.
- [ ] Sources near claims.
- [ ] Page limit respected.
- [ ] Fairness language is properly qualified.

### Takeaways

A good memo tells the reader what matters, why, and what would change the conclusion.
