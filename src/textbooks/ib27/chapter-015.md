## Chapter 12 — Strategic Fit

### Learning objectives

- Build a strategic thesis.
- Identify falsifiers.
- Relate strategy to financial outputs.

### Why it matters

A transaction thesis should explain both why the deal exists and what could disprove it.

### Explanation

The strategic thesis has three layers:

1. Portfolio breadth.
2. Commercial and service reach.
3. Operating leverage.

Only the third is directly quantified through management’s $35 million annual run-rate guide. The first two remain unquantified analyst judgments. No customer-overlap, qualification, or revenue-synergy evidence was built.

### Project connection

The ratio is reported within disclosed adviser fairness ranges, subject to the cited 424B3 annexes and advisers. The base operating-proxy case is accretive, while no synergy is dilutive.

### Worked example — Analyst judgment

> ACLS/VECO is a breadth-and-scale combination across complementary semiconductor equipment categories. The economics work if recovery and synergy delivery offset the dilution created by issuing ACLS shares.

### Common mistakes

- Listing synergies without timing.
- Ignoring integration costs.
- Treating strategic complementarity as proof of value creation.
- Presenting commercial reach or service breadth as measured outputs.

### Checklist

- [ ] State the strategic rationale.
- [ ] Quantify only what is quantified.
- [ ] Name at least three falsifiers.
- [ ] Avoid regulatory certainty.
- [ ] Cite adviser fairness analyses precisely.

### Takeaways

Strategy is strongest when linked to measurable drivers and explicit failure conditions.
