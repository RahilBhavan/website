## Chapter 11 — Semiconductor Process Concepts

### Learning objectives

- Map basic process steps.
- Explain why process tools are specialized.
- Avoid technical overclaiming.

### Why it matters

Interviewers test whether an analyst understands the business behind the numbers.

### Explanation

A simplified process sequence is:

```text
deposit → pattern → etch → clean → implant → anneal → inspect
```

This is educational context, not a complete wafer-flow model.

### Project connection

The combination spans neighboring process capabilities, but the project does not provide a node-by-node revenue split, customer qualification schedule, or full capacity model.

### Worked example — Illustration

A reticle field of 26 mm × 33 mm has:

\[
26\times33=858\text{mm}^2
\]

This explains why some large products use multiple dies. It does not prove a specific ACLS or VECO product advantage.

### Common mistakes

- Connecting every company to the latest semiconductor theme.
- Confusing process relevance with revenue exposure.
- Claiming wafer-capacity effects without a capacity model.

### Checklist

- [ ] Define technical terms plainly.
- [ ] Identify source-backed claims.
- [ ] Distinguish educational context from project evidence.

### Takeaways

Technical fluency improves judgment only when bounded by evidence.
