Result 1: size

From Dec 26, 2022 to the week of Oct 5, 2026, the six assets saw $1.01 trillion of gross mints and redemptions on Ethereum. The table splits it by asset. Net on Ethereum is not the same as net globally (chapter 5), so read the net column with care.

Result 2: the rate episodes

The figure shows each asset's average weekly net issuance before and during each easing episode, in standard deviations of that asset's own normal week.

In the first episode (Sep 2024 to Dec 2024), USDC moved to +0.51 σ and USDT to +0.48 σ. PYUSD moved to −0.22 σ, BUIDL to −0.20 σ and OUSG to −0.35 σ. In the second (Sep 2025 to Dec 2025), USDC was −0.04, USDT +0.38, PYUSD +0.87, BUIDL −0.31 and OUSG +0.03.

The first episode fits the "stablecoins up, funds down" story from chapter 6 for the larger assets. The second does not: PYUSD is the strongest and USDC is flat. BUIDL had its lowest window of its history in 2025. So the honest summary is mixed.

Result 3: where the supply sits

Ethereum has most of USDC and PYUSD but a minority of USDT. That is why the log-based gross flows describe USDC and PYUSD better than USDT, and why net issuance for those three uses all-chain supply.

Tether's own Issue and Redeem events on Tron cover part of that gap. Since January 2023 they show $71.0B issued and $13.3B redeemed, $57.7B net. That net is within about 6% of the change in Tron's USDT supply (DefiLlama), which is the check that the log reading is right. Tron is reported beside the Ethereum totals, not added to them, so the headline stays Ethereum only. Solana, Base and other USDC chains are still not read from logs.
