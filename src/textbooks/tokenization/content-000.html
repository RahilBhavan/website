How to use this book

Your goal is to independently explain, calculate, investigate and challenge a system. You have not demonstrated mastery merely because an explanation feels familiar. Begin each chapter with its attempt, preserve your answer, study the chapter, diagnose one gap, then solve the fresh check without looking at the solutions. Consult the answer appendix only after submitting your own work to yourself or a tutor.

Each subject is its own loop: attempt -> diagnose -> targeted study -> fresh attempt -> feedback -> delayed recall. You can complete a loop independently. Each begins with the prerequisite ideas it needs. For a first pass, use the printed order: tokenomics, RWA, then tokenization. Later, revisit only the loop where your evidence shows a gap.

Use 45-75 minutes per session as a flexible starting estimate. Spend enough time reading to make an attempt; then calculate, explain or design. At the effort limit, record one unresolved gap. Do not keep reading to avoid testing yourself. Suggested delayed checks are two days and one week later; these are study suggestions, not scheduled automations.

Keep a small learning record: target capability; original attempt; exact uncertain step; help used; fresh unaided answer; result; next gap. Ask a tutor for one hint before a full solution. If you need the solution, use a different problem for the independent check.

A working mastery threshold: at least 80% on a loop exam, every essential safety distinction correct, and a defensible capstone. This is a study rubric, not a professional credential. Long-term expertise also requires repeated work with actual contracts, documents, market data, operational incidents and expert feedback.

Orientation: three lenses on one system

A token is an entry in a ledger governed by rules. A blockchain maintains an agreed history of state changes among participants under a particular consensus system. A smart contract is code executed under the chain's rules. An address identifies an account; control of its key can authorize actions. A signature proves authorization by a key under the relevant scheme, not a person's legal identity or economic entitlement.

A coin is commonly a chain's native asset; a token commonly runs in a contract. Usage varies. Fungible units are interchangeable within the same class. A non-fungible token has a distinct identifier. Fungibility does not imply a stable price. A token can represent access, voting power, a redeemable claim or a financial interest; inspect the actual rules and documents.

Consider a fictional property company. It owns an apartment building and issues digital units. Tokenomics examines incentives, fees and distribution. RWA analysis examines the company, debt, property and investor rights. Tokenization examines the register, wallet permissions, transfers, servicing and redemption. None can substitute for the other two.

A transaction may be technically valid while being legally disputed. A legal right may survive a technical outage. Record both. Ask four questions whenever someone says a token is "backed": backed by what; held by whom; for whose benefit; enforceable how?

Mathematics you will use

A stock is measured at an instant: outstanding units, reserves, debt. A flow is measured over a period: annual fees, monthly issuance, daily withdrawals. Never divide a monthly flow by an annual flow without converting units.

Percentage change = (new - old) / old. A fall from 100 to 60 is 40%; returning from 60 to 100 requires 66.67%. APR is an annualized simple rate. If a periodic rate r compounds n times per year, APY = (1 + r)^n - 1. A 1% monthly rate implies 12% simple annualization and about 12.68% compounded annual return, assuming reinvestment at the same rate.

Present value discounts future cash: PV = future cash / (1 + discount rate)^years. A risky $110 payment in a year is worth $100 at a 10% discount rate if that rate appropriately represents the risk. Choosing the rate is a judgment, not a calculator fact. For uncertain outcomes, expected value is the probability-weighted average; it is not the outcome you are guaranteed to receive.

Balance-sheet identity: assets = liabilities + residual equity. If assets fall, debt does not automatically shrink. Residual holders absorb losses first under a simplified senior-debt structure. Liquidity means being able to meet payments or trade on acceptable terms. Solvency means assets can cover obligations under the relevant valuation and legal assumptions. A solvent vehicle can lack cash today; a cash-rich vehicle can still have liabilities exceeding its assets.

Diagnostic: explain why a $1 token price does not tell you whether a project is cheap. Explain why an on-chain transfer does not establish ownership of a house. Calculate the return needed after a 50% loss. If these are difficult, finish the orientation before moving on. Answer guide: unit count and rights matter; title and legal linkage matter; recovery requires 100%.

Learning loop 01

Tokenomics

Who gets paid, who gets diluted, and who has the power to change it?

Outcome and entry test

You will reconstruct a token's supply, incentives, cash flows and control, then test whether its design survives adverse behavior. Tokenomics means the economic rules around a token: who receives it, why anyone holds or spends it, what changes supply, and how decisions are made.

Before reading: a network earns $2 million in annual fees, issues $8 million of reward tokens at today's price, and has a $100 million circulating market capitalization. Is its token economically attractive? Write a provisional answer and identify five missing facts. Your later answer should resist turning those three numbers into an investment conclusion.
