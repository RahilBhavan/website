## Chapter 6 — Comparable Historical Analysis

### Learning objectives

- Build historical trend tables.
- Distinguish current and mid-cycle contribution.
- Construct explicit EV bridges.

### Why it matters

A cyclical company can look structurally weak or strong depending on the selected year.

### Explanation

Historical formulas include:

\[
Revenue\ growth_t=Revenue_t/Revenue_{t-1}-1
\]

\[
Operating\ margin=OI/Revenue
\]

Frozen contribution outputs:

| Measure | ACLS | VECO |
|---|---:|---:|
| FY2025 revenue contribution | 55.81% | 44.19% |
| FY2025 operating-income contribution | 76.97% | 23.03% |
| Mid-cycle operating-income contribution | 74.17% | 25.83% |
| Market-cap contribution | 62.64% | 37.36% |

The six-peer set is AMAT, LRCX, KLAC, ONTO, FORM, and COHU.

### Project connection

The negotiated 58.48%/41.52% ownership split falls between several economic lenses rather than matching one automatically.

### Worked example — Illustration

For two companies with EVs of $1,100 million and $700 million and EBITDA of $100 million each:

- Multiples: 11.0x and 7.0x.
- Median: 9.0x.

### Common mistakes

- Comparing trough OI with peak revenue.
- Using pasted EVs.
- Calling a median fair value.
- Changing the peer set silently.
- Confusing market-cap contribution with market-capitalization amounts.

### Checklist

- [ ] Peer set frozen.
- [ ] Fiscal periods labeled.
- [ ] EV bridges visible.
- [ ] Current and normalized measures separated.
- [ ] Contribution labels use the correct terminology.

### Takeaways

Contribution analysis informs judgment; it does not mechanically determine fairness.
