## Chapter 28 — Communication Discipline and Overclaiming

### Learning objectives

- Replace unsupported language with evidence-bounded language.
- Handle skeptical questions.
- Distinguish confidence from certainty.

### Why it matters

Credibility is lost faster through one exaggerated claim than through one acknowledged limitation.

### Explanation

Prefer:

> “The workbook records a +10.72% spread as of 2026-08-05 under its stated convention.”

Avoid:

> “The market gives us 10.72% upside.”

Prefer:

> “The base operating-proxy case is accretive.”

Avoid:

> “The transaction will be accretive.”

Prefer:

> “The project does not handicap SAMR.”

Avoid:

> “SAMR approval is likely.”

Prefer:

> “The portfolio breadth and commercial-reach thesis is an unquantified analyst judgment; no customer-overlap, qualification, or revenue-synergy evidence was built.”

### Project connection

The claim ledger contains explicit non-claims for regression, synergies, PPA, audio, and fresh data.

### Worked example

Question: “Did you build a bottoms-up synergy model?”  
Answer:

> No. I stress-tested management’s $35 million annual run-rate guide, targeted within 24 months with the majority in the first 12 months. A bottoms-up build would require headcount, facilities, procurement, systems, timing, and costs-to-achieve data that are not publicly available in the frozen scope.

### Common mistakes

- Using “proves,” “guarantees,” “current,” “latest,” “complete,” or “fair” without qualification.
- Omitting model scope from résumé bullets.
- Confusing polished formatting with evidence.
- Calling the annual run-rate guide realized savings.

### Checklist

- [ ] Use precise verbs.
- [ ] Add dates.
- [ ] Label assumptions.
- [ ] Name unresolved gaps.
- [ ] Qualify strategic judgments.

### Takeaways

Responsible communication makes the analysis more persuasive, not less.
