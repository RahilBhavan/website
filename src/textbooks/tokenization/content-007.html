Choose what the token represents

After this chapter: Distinguish underlying assets from vault shares and interface behavior.

Watch for this mistake: One share must always equal one underlying asset unit.

Specify the object before choosing the chain. Is it a unit of currency, an individual claim, a fund share, an invoice or a permission? Can units substitute for one another? Does one identifier represent one asset or a class? What must remain private? Who is entitled to make changes?

ERC-20 defines a common interface for fungible token balances and transfers [1]. ERC-721 defines an interface for non-fungible tokens with distinct identifiers [2]. ERC-1155 supports multiple token types within one contract [3]. ERC-4626 standardizes tokenized vault share operations around a single underlying ERC-20 asset [4]. These are interoperability choices, not proof of asset quality, title or regulatory compliance.

Worked vault model: a vault has 1,200 asset units and 1,000 shares. Ignoring fees and rounding, each share represents 1.2 asset units. A deposit of 120 asset units mints 100 shares. Assets become 1,320 and shares 1,100; ratio remains 1.2. Issuing 120 shares instead would dilute existing holders in this model. A deployed standard has more detailed rules for previews, limits and rounding; do not implement it from this paragraph.

Decimals are display scaling over integer balances. At six decimals, one displayed unit corresponds to 1,000,000 base units. Decimal precision does not create extra economic value. Rounding errors matter when many small transactions or extreme exchange rates accumulate.

Fresh check Z1: assets 2,000, shares 1,600. Ignoring fees and rounding, calculate shares for a 250-asset deposit and assets for a 100-share redemption. Explain why the standard alone cannot establish immediate withdrawal availability.

Reveal this case’s solution

At 1.5 assets per share, the deposit receives 100 shares. The interface does not guarantee profitable assets, legal title to off-chain property, solvency or immediate withdrawal capacity.

Check the core idea

What is the difference between deposit and mint, or withdraw and redeem?

Deposit and withdraw specify asset amounts. Mint and redeem specify share amounts. Fees, rounding, previews and limits affect actual execution under the relevant rules.

Loop 03 / Chapter 14

Model the lifecycle and its invariants

After this chapter: Specify lifecycle states and reconcile one economic operation across retries.

Watch for this mistake: A successful request submission means issuance completed.

Write the lifecycle as states and permitted transitions. A subscription can be requested, eligibility-checked, cash-pending, cash-confirmed, issued, rejected or refunded. A redemption can be requested, validated, queued, funded, settled or failed. Distinguish a requested operation from a completed one.

An invariant is a condition that must remain true across valid operations. Examples: no unauthorized mint; no duplicated issuance for one subscription; no transfer to an ineligible recipient under the design; no completed redemption both paid and left as an unrestricted spendable claim. Define backing invariants in matching units and by the legally appropriate valuation; a share count need not equal dollar NAV.

Worked reconciliation: start with 1,000 units, issue 200, cancel 50, transfer 100 between holders. Ending supply is 1,150, not 1,250. Internal transfers change distribution, not supply. Then reconcile the official register and cash records to the ledger; agreement between two software views is insufficient if both omitted the same bank reversal.

Retries require stable operation identity. If a payment webhook arrives twice, one payment should create one issuance. Restarting after a crash should resume from durable state. If a bank transfer is reversed after minting, a compensating procedure is needed; pretending the original event never happened destroys the audit trail.

Which evidence must distinguish requested, confirmed and issued?

The durable request and eligibility record, confirmed cash evidence, and the linked chain transaction under the application's finality policy.

Loop 03 / Chapter 15

Identity, permissions and privacy

After this chapter: Separate identity evidence, transfer permissions and recovery authority.

Watch for this mistake: An allowlist automatically solves compliance and privacy.

A wallet address is not a complete identity record. Onboarding may verify a person or entity, beneficial owners, eligibility, sanctions exposure and associated wallets. The required checks depend on the product and law. A permissioned token's recipient rules must apply at the actual transfer boundary, including interactions with contracts and delegated spenders.

Separate identity evidence from permission status. A system can keep sensitive documents off-chain and use a revocable eligibility record on-chain, though the linkage can still expose personal information. Public ledgers preserve information widely; hashes of predictable or sensitive data are not automatically private. Minimize what must be published and define retention, correction and access controls.

Lost-key recovery presents a tradeoff. If no one can reassign balances, an investor may lose practical access. If an administrator can freeze or reissue, that power can be abused or compromised. Document the legal authority, verification, approvals, logging and prevention of double claims. "Non-custodial" key control does not eliminate other administrators in the product.

Permissioned-token standards such as ERC-3643 describe identity and compliance-related interfaces [9]. Their implementation and surrounding legal processes still need investigation. A transfer hook cannot verify a fact no trusted system supplies.

Fresh check Z3: an investor passes onboarding, then becomes ineligible. Which operations should the design permit: receipt, transfer, redemption, distribution or recovery? There is no universal answer. Write a policy, justify it from assumed terms and identify who can update the status and how mistakes are corrected.
