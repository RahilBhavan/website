Meeting prep

Why you need thisPractise these out loud. Each answer is short and true to the data.

Why this pool?

It is the busiest WETH/USDT pool and was the only regularly sandwiched pool in the first nine-pool scan. A later all-pool scan showed that list missed USDC/WETH 0.01%, which is busier and sandwiched about as often, so the fair claim is “the busiest WETH/USDT pool”. The pattern is the 0.01% fee tier.

Does it hold outside blue chips?

On the most-sandwiched memecoin pool that week (SIMD/WETH 0.01%): 240 sandwiches, $1,330 lost, 95% of it by one automated buyer, and victims keep 73% under the mechanism on average. Same mechanism effect, much smaller dollars.

How do you know they are sandwiches?

A published rule, three robustness tiers, evidence for 30 random hits, and 5 hits checked on Etherscan. All held up; the strictest tier still has 178 sandwiches.

Isn't “attacker net ≤ 0” just your Theorem 3.1?

Yes, and it checks the implementation rather than adding a new result. The new parts are the victim numbers, the 98% tolerance finding and the range of protection on real order flow.

Why not 100% with the same orders?

Phase 2 still lets an order ahead of the victim move the price. With the attacker's executed prices as limits the back-run is dropped and half the front-run still trades. Random tie-breaking raises the share to 75%.

How did you treat Uniswap's average-price limits?

As marginal limits. That under-fills orders and sets the lower bound. A version of the mechanism for average-price limits is the open question I'd like to ask about.

What about fees?

Phase 2 keeps the 0.01% pool fee. Phase 1 was run with and without it; the result is the same to the reported precision.

Concentrated liquidity?

v3 is constant-product within each range, and the price still rises with each unit bought (increasing marginal cost), which the proofs need. JIT liquidity inside a block is outside the paper's model.

What would you do next?

More weeks and pools; multi-pool and cross-block sandwiches; an empirical game of attacker and builder responses; and mechanisms for average-price limits.

Glossary and reading list

AMM
Automated market maker: a contract that holds two tokens and trades against anyone by a fixed rule.
Arbitrage resilience
No group of orders can make a risk-free profit from the mechanism.
Average price
Total paid ÷ total received for one trade.
Back-run
The attacker's trade right after the victim, closing its position.
Builder
The party that orders transactions into a block to sell to the proposer.
Counterfactual
An exact replay of the block with something changed, here the attacker removed.
EGTA
Empirical game-theoretic analysis: estimate a game's payoffs by simulation and solve it.
Front-run
The attacker's trade right before the victim, in the same direction.
IC
Incentive compatibility: truthful reporting is the best strategy.
JIT liquidity
Liquidity added just before a swap and removed just after.
Marginal price
The price of the next tiny unit; the slope of the curve.
MEV
Maximal extractable value: profit from choosing, inserting or reordering transactions in a block.
Minimum output
The least a swap accepts; below it the transaction reverts.
r0
The pool's marginal price at the start of the block.
sqrtPriceX96
Uniswap v3's stored price: √price × 2⁹⁶, an integer.
Tick
A 0.01% price step; liquidity ranges start and end on ticks.
UP
Uniform pricing: every executed order trades at one price.
wLE
Weak local efficiency: no reasonable order is left unfilled when the ending price still suits it.
