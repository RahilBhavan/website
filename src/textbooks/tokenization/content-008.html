Loop 03 / Chapter 16

Settlement, oracles and interoperability

After this chapter: State exactly which settlement domain is atomic.

Watch for this mistake: A token transfer makes an external bank payment atomic too.

Atomicity means a linked operation completes fully or has no partial effect within the specified execution domain. Two contracts on the same chain can participate in one transaction; an external bank transfer normally cannot be rolled back by that transaction. Specify the boundary of any claim to atomic settlement.

Worked DvP design: a buyer locks eligible payment tokens and a seller locks eligible asset tokens. One transaction exchanges both or reverts. This can address principal risk inside that system. It still depends on payment-token quality, eligibility, contract correctness, chain behavior and legal recognition. If the seller receives a dollar stablecoin, receiving it is not identical to receiving a bank deposit.

An oracle supplies external information such as NAV, interest rates or payment confirmation. Ask who publishes it, how often, in what units, with what signature and what happens when it is stale or contested. Price accuracy and asset existence are different problems; a correct gold price cannot prove the gold is in custody.

A bridge often locks or burns on one chain and issues a representation on another. It adds verification, custody or messaging assumptions. Define globally conserved claims: if 100 original units are locked and 100 wrapped units circulate, that should not be counted as 200 independent claims on assets. Cross-chain recovery must address reorganization, delayed messages and duplicate processing.

Fresh check Z4: a system advertises instant DvP but sends the buyer's bank payment on Monday and transfers the token Tuesday. Identify the unlinked obligations, principal-risk exposure and the evidence required to substantiate any narrower settlement claim.

Close the chapter. Try a new case.

A buyer wires dollars on Friday and receives tokens Monday. Is this atomic DvP? Identify the exposure.

Reveal this case’s solution

The legs settle separately. One party may perform first and face counterparty and timing exposure. A token record alone cannot make the earlier wire reversible or contingent.

Check the core idea

What does all-or-nothing execution cover in a single-chain exchange?

Only the linked operations within the defined execution domain. External cash, legal recognition, identity and payment-token quality require separate treatment.

Loop 03 / Chapter 17

Security, administration and operational recovery

After this chapter: Design observable recovery behavior for failures and privileged actions.

Watch for this mistake: A contract audit proves every deployed configuration is safe forever.

Threat modeling starts with valuable assets, trust boundaries and adversaries. Include external attackers, compromised insiders, honest operator mistakes, unavailable counterparties and corrupted data. Distinguish code flaws from design risks: perfect code can faithfully implement an insolvent promise.

Review mint/burn authorization, upgrades, pause powers, allowances, external calls, rounding and oracle inputs. For vaults, examine how deposits, donations and share pricing interact; OpenZeppelin's documentation discusses inflation-attack mechanics and defenses [10]. A contract audit is scoped evidence, not a permanent guarantee. Its reviewed version, deployment configuration and privileged roles must match the system being assessed.

Administration is part of the security model. Multisignatures, role separation, timelocks, key storage and emergency procedures can reduce risks while slowing recovery. A pause may stop a theft while trapping users; a forced transfer may fix an error while weakening resistance to seizure. Explain the tradeoff in the product terms and operating plan.

Worked incident: an oracle publishes NAV multiplied by 100 because of a decimal mismatch. New shares are mispriced. A resilient design rejects implausible values or suspends affected operations, preserves evidence, reconciles affected claims and follows an authorized correction process. A price bound is not enough on its own: real market moves or stale values can also trigger it.

Loop 03 / Chapter 18

Decide whether tokenization is worth doing

After this chapter: Compare a tokenized design with a credible conventional alternative.

Watch for this mistake: Successful token minting proves that tokenization improved the product.

Compare a tokenized design against a good conventional system, not an intentionally poor baseline. Potential gains include shared state, automated rules, programmable settlement and easier integration. Costs include implementation, assurance, custody, fragmented liquidity, privacy, legal linkage and operational dependence. The FSB's risk framework [11] is useful for organizing vulnerabilities; its 2024 market observations are historical, not current market measurements.

Define a measurable problem. Example hypothesis: a shared register reduces reconciliation exceptions for a small institutional fund. Pilot with synthetic data first. Measure exceptions per subscription, time to resolve them, cost per completed lifecycle, failed transfers and recovery time. Faster successful transfers alone do not establish lower total operating cost.

Use a staged design: paper rights map; ledger and cash model; simulated lifecycle; sandbox integration; independent review; carefully authorized pilot. Do not issue an actual asset merely to learn a token standard. A database simulation can reveal a missing redemption transition before any smart contract exists.

Fresh check Z6: an institution proposes a token for a fund with 12 investors who transfer twice a year. Build the strongest case for and against tokenization. Specify the observation that would make you stop the project. A reasonable answer may favor conventional infrastructure.
