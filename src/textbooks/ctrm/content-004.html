Cargo and stock

After this chapter you can
  • Follow a shipment through its states and see which ones write stock.
  • Compute transit loss, landed cost and weighted average cost.
  • Say why stock is a sum of movements, not a number you edit.

A shipment is one vessel lift. It carries quantities, dates, documents and position from loading to discharge.

Key quantities: B/L quantity (the carrier's receipt at loading), outturn (received at the terminal). Documents carry a checklist (received, checked, discrepancy, accepted). A discrepancy blocks LC acceptance until cleared. Files are versioned and never deleted. Vessel position and ETA come from an AIS adapter when available, or from the agent's report entered by Operations. The system records which.

Stock is a sum

The balance at any location on any date is the sum of movements up to that date. To fix a mistake, post a reversing movement with a reason. That is how the system answers "what did we hold on 14 October?" months later.

Transit loss

loss_mt = bl_qty − outturn   loss_pct = loss_mt / bl_qty x 100

5,000 loaded, 4,990 discharged: 10 MT, 0.20%. A loss above the product's tolerance is flagged for review, so claims against carriers or surveyors can be pursued.

Landed cost

The cost of oil in the depot is everything paid to get it there. Unit cost at the depot = landed cost / outturn.

Weighted average cost

Cnew = (Qold x Cold + Qin x Cin) / (Qold + Qin)

The depot holds 3,000 MT at $1,655.00 and receives 4,990 MT at $1,633.36. New cost = $1,641.49/MT. Tanks commingle oil, so tracking each cargo separately has no physical meaning. Tonnes leaving carry the average at that moment.

Stock belongs to the legal entity that bought it. Selling between entities is an intercompany sale with its own invoice. The ledger never moves stock between entities silently.

Money rule

Costs flow through inventory only. Statutory inventory accounting is Odoo's. ADR-0006 (proposed) sets the edge shipments → inventory so every custody movement goes through one posting path.

    Answer

Chapter 9

Letters of credit and payments

After this chapter you can
  • Describe an LC's states and when the clock starts.
  • Compute due date, financing cost and headroom.

A letter of credit (LC) is a bank's promise to pay the seller against correct documents. An LC at sight pays on presentation. A usance LC pays after a tenor, for example 60 days. The CTRM tracks the operational register. The bank ledger and payables stay in Odoo.

due_date = trigger_date + tenor_days (rolled to the next business day)
financing_cost = amount x annual_rate x days / 360
headroom = amount x (1 + tolerance) − Σ drawings

Example: financing on the purchase value 7,661,054.50 at 8% for 60 days = 102,147.39 USD. An LC of $7,000,000 with 10% tolerance and nothing drawn has 7,700,000.00 headroom.

Why tolerance exists

The LC is usually opened before pricing is complete, so it is sized on an estimated price. Tolerance covers quantity and price variation. The supplier's invoice may be provisional at loading and final after pricing, with a balancing payment.

The maturity ladder

It groups accepted, unsettled LCs by days to maturity (overdue, 0 to 7, 8 to 30, 31 to 60, over 60) and compares them with cash and receivables in the same buckets. About two months of purchases are in LCs at any time, near $50M.

Instruments come in four kinds: lc_sight, lc_usance, advance, open_account. An advance is matched against the final invoice when pricing completes.
