Which new names are worth a visit?

The logs carry over a thousand new registrable names a minute. The fetcher manages about four. The name filter decides which ones get a visit, so it sets both SSRD's workload and the share of scam sites it could ever catch.

What you'll learn
Public suffixes, eTLD+1, punycode, keyword matching and a character n-gram classifier.
Why it matters
A site the filter drops is never fetched, so filter recall caps everything after it.

3.1 The part of a name an operator chooses

The operator chooses one part of a hostname: the label just left of the public suffix. Everything left of that is subdomains they can make up for free. Everything right is a suffix anyone can register under. The Public Suffix List (PSL) says where the suffix ends. It isn't always the last dot: co.uk is a suffix, and so are private suffixes such as vercel.app, where every customer gets their own name.

3.2 Punycode: what xn-- means

DNS only carries ASCII. Internationalized names are stored in punycode, ASCII starting with xn--. xn--e1afmkfd.xn--p1ai is пример.рф, and инвест-капитал becomes xn----8sbafpqeomm1aunf. A keyword such as инвест can only match after decoding, so names.to_unicode decodes each label first. The SPEC also says to judge language from the page, not the domain, because CBR seeds are mostly English-named .com sites. Only 102 of the 2,601 from 2026 are .ru.

3.3 The keyword filter, and why it wasn't enough

The first filter was a keyword list in four groups in config/ssrd.toml: investment terms in English, transliterated and Cyrillic Russian, recovery terms, and bait (regulators and brands such as nydfs, musk, gazprom). Terms of four or more letters match anywhere in the label. Shorter ones, and a list of boundary_terms, must stand alone between separators or digits, so fx matches fx-pro but not effect. Infrastructure suffixes such as amazonaws.com are skipped.

Then it was measured. Of 5,020 CBR-listed domains from 2026, the keyword list passed 1,650 (33%). Most scam names are invented brands that contain no investment word at all. That finding is what led to a model.

3.4 A character n-gram classifier

The model doesn't look for words. It looks at how a name is spelled. Wrap the label in start and end markers and cut it into every 2-, 3-, 4- and 5-character piece. For fx-bot, the 2-grams are ^f fx x- -b bo ot t$ and the 3-grams are ^fx fx- x-b -bo bot ot$. Each piece is hashed into one of 220 buckets (the "hashing trick", so there's no vocabulary to store), and the counts are scaled to length 1. A logistic regression learns one weight per bucket. The score is 1 / (1 + e−z), where z is the bias plus the weighted sum.

Positives are CBR-listed labels. Negatives are the CT background sample from chapter 2. Training needs scikit-learn, but scoring is a few lines of pure Python with mmh3, so the ingest container stays small.

The split is by time, not at random

The model trains on CBR names listed before 2026-07-01 and is tested on names listed after. A random split would let near-duplicate names from the same campaign sit on both sides and inflate the score. A temporal split asks the question SSRD actually faces: does what we learned from past scams catch future ones?

Two numbers matter here, and precision isn't one of them. Recall is coverage: the share of real scam names that get a visit. Pass rate is workload: the share of all names sent to the fetcher. The filter doesn't have to be precise, because fingerprinting decides later. It has to keep enough scams in while fitting the fetcher's capacity.

One caution about the 0.55

The 0.93 threshold was picked by looking at this same test table, so 0.55 is a slightly optimistic estimate of recall on truly new names. It isn't wrong, but it isn't clean either. The clean check is the next monthly retrain: score names CBR lists after Oct 17 with the frozen threshold, and report that number too. Chapter 8.5 has the general rule.

A priority score orders the queue when it backs up: points for the model score above 0.9, one per keyword group, two more for bait terms, and one for a TLD common in the seeds (online, live, pro, top and so on).

Check yourself

What is the registrable domain of login.secure.trade-elon.online, and which groups match?
trade-elon.online. trade matches (investment). elon is a boundary term, and it stands between a hyphen and the end of the label, so bait matches too. That gives a high priority.
Why is precision the wrong number to optimize for this filter?
Its job is to keep scams in while staying under fetch capacity. Fingerprints and the two-family rule decide what is a match. A precise but low-recall filter would cap the whole system's catch rate.
Why hash n-grams into buckets instead of keeping a vocabulary?
No vocabulary to store or keep in sync, a fixed-size model, and pure-Python scoring in the ingest container. The cost is occasional collisions between n-grams, which a linear model tolerates.
A friend says "just split the CBR names randomly into train and test." What goes wrong?
Campaign siblings (brandx-pro, brandx-trade) land on both sides, so the test measures memory, not generalization. The temporal split asks whether the past predicts the future.
